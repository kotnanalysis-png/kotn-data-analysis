Operational questions

High activity can generate hypotheses about pickup volume, but a bid is not a confirmed purchase. The dataset contains no pickup appointments, actual collection durations, staff movements, invoices or verified winning accounts. We therefore do not estimate physical pickup burdens from bid counts.

Research focus

Our current aim is to measure behavioural patterns within publicly observable bidding histories, not reconstruct pickup logistics or spending.

What a large pickup could mean

A bidder leading 100–250 items is not necessarily collecting that many items. If an account eventually wins a large group of listings, fulfillment workload depends on the number and size of items, retrieval method, batching and staffing.

Staff-minutes are not customer wait times. Four staff working concurrently or a pre-staged bulk order could change the queue dramatically.

Queue-impact hypothesis

With 50 customers present and 100 more arriving, a large pickup could increase waits if staff are shared and workload exceeds available processing capacity. Conversely, separate fulfillment staff, staggered arrivals and batching could substantially reduce the impact. We have no timestamped pickup observations from which to estimate actual delay.

Published pickup arrangements

KOTN has publicly described pickup as first-come, first-served, with scheduling disabled. This does not establish whether any unpublished bulk processes exist. Verify current wording directly with the official Payment and Pickup page before making a public assertion.
