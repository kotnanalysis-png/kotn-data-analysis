Operational questions: evidence boundaries

Bid histories do not identify purchaser identity, completed payment, pickup order, storage occupancy, or special pickup treatment. No claim about a user’s physical purchase volume or collection arrangement can be derived from these CSVs.

For repeat large-scale accounts, relevant operational questions remain whether leading positions ultimately result in collections and whether standard pickup policies are applied consistently. The current data cannot answer those questions.

Pickup operations: what data can and cannot show

The November 2 catalogue includes 39,024 positive-bid listings and 4,680 distinct provisional leading account identifiers. These are not pickup counts, completed purchases, collected goods, or independent physical persons.

The historical pickup thought experiment and its stated assumptions are retained in the archive below. No inference about preferential collection or warehouse treatment follows from bid frequency.

Operational questions for future evidence

A verified operations study would need independently observed fulfilled sales, pickup appointment rules, and privacy-respecting aggregate customer flow. Those data are not available in the public bid export.

What a large pickup could mean

A bidder leading 100–250 items is not necessarily collecting that many items. If an account eventually wins a large group of listings, fulfillment workload depends on the number and size of items, retrieval method, batching and staffing.

Staff-minutes are not customer wait times. Four staff working concurrently or a pre-staged bulk order could change the queue dramatically.

Queue-impact hypothesis

With 50 customers present and 100 more arriving, a large pickup could increase waits if staff are shared and workload exceeds available processing capacity. Conversely, separate fulfillment staff, staggered arrivals and batching could substantially reduce the impact. We have no timestamped pickup observations from which to estimate actual delay.

Published pickup arrangements

KOTN has publicly described pickup as first-come, first-served, with scheduling disabled. This does not establish whether any unpublished bulk processes exist. Verify current wording directly with the official Payment and Pickup page before making a public assertion.
